Don’t ask for an image. Ask for a drawing machine.

One image is easy for Claude. The trouble starts with a series: every image comes out slightly different. So I ask Claude to write a program that draws every image by the same rules.

DimensionsA product table, a drawing or a brochure.
RulesAngle, light, colour and line, decided once.
ReviewClaude looks at the result and fixes it before I see it.
SeriesA new product is a new row of data, not a new drawing.

This is what a drawing machine looks like

A beam is three numbers. Change a number and the image redraws. That is how BCE’s whole product catalogue was made.

01 / 05

GRK: five sites, one visual language

A data centre’s whole infrastructure, step by step. The image builds as you scroll.

Lock the rules first, and the fifth site is as easy as the first.

02 / 05

BCE: a deck that opens as you scroll

Scroll, and the deck comes apart layer by layer: boards, beams, piers and base. One image shows what you buy and how it goes together.

One drawing machine, dozens of products. This is where the method pays for itself.

03 / 05

Finmatec: the whole line, one station at a time

A pet food line from reception to plate freezer. As you scroll, the camera moves from station to station and opens up the machines behind it.

The strength of SVG is not the surface but the structure. Parts have names, and they can move.

04 / 05

Luotokoti: a walk through the house

The house turns and opens as you scroll: roof, floor plan and decks. Live on luotokoti.fi.

Scroll is the best controller: the user sets the pace, the image tells the story.

05 / 05

Kaskipuu: a window, cut open

A window corner with its profiles, glass and seals. As you scroll, the model comes apart. This is 3D, but the idea is the same: profiles are dimensions, and the image is drawn from them.

When you draw with numbers, moving from SVG to 3D needs no new way of working.

Where it falls short

Anything organicPeople, animals, landscapes. Shapes without rules are still a job for Illustrator or an image model.
One-off imagesNeed one picture? Just draw it. The machine pays off once you need a series.
Vague briefsEvery round of iteration costs tokens. Ten minutes on the brief saves an hour of back and forth.
Judging tasteClaude can check proportions and dimensions. Whether it looks good is still your call.

Your first prompt

Draw [product] as an SVG from these dimensions: [table]. Don’t draw it by hand: write a generator. Isometric view, light from the top left, colours from [brand palette]. Check the render yourself before you show me.
